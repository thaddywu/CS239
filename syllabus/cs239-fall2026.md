## General Description

Software agents are changing how software is designed, implemented, tested, and maintained. Through paper discussions, this course introduces the foundations, techniques, and applications of agentic software engineering. Topics include how agents plan and coordinate, use tools, generate and modify code, test and repair software, support program analysis and formal verification, and how the correctness and safety of agentic systems can be evaluated, monitored, and enforced.

Most class meetings focus on one research paper and are led by students.

Bring your own machine and API tokens.

## Lead Paper Discussion

Each student will lead one paper discussion, either alone or with one other student. A typical meeting takes ~50 minutes for the guided reading and ~25 minutes for Q&A.

Every paper discussion assumes that everyone has read the assigned paper before class and is prepared to ask and answer questions.

- The discussion uses an annotated PDF and prepared questions rather than slides. Open-ended questions are encouraged when they help guide the discussion.
- Use the whiteboard, when helpful, to explain the paper's core methodology with a concrete example.
- Expect questions from the class and instructors and be prepared to respond and guide the conversation.

Paper discussion assignments will be finalized by **Sunday, October 4 (Week 1)**.

## "Here's a Cool Agentic Thing I Saw"

On **Tuesday, November 24 (Week 9)**, each student will have five minutes to introduce one agentic system, paper, or technical idea that the rest of the class should know about. It may come from a neighboring area such as science, hardware design, robotics, security, or human–AI interaction. Bring one useful visual rather than a full slide deck. A figure, a live demo, a system diagram, or a short excerpt is encouraged.

## Individual Research Proposal

The final project is an individual research proposal. The goal is to develop a technically specific and testable research idea, not to finish a large implementation.

The proposal should use the [ACM conference format](https://www.acm.org/publications/proceedings-template) and contain at most three pages of main text; references do not count toward the page limit. Proposal quality will be judged by the following:

- A clear problem definition and a concrete motivating example.
- A summary of related work and the unresolved research gap.
- A technically specific proposed approach.
- A thorough evaluation plan, including baselines, benchmarks or metrics.
- Clear writing, feasible scope, and sufficient consideration of risks and limitations.
